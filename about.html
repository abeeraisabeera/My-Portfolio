A little about how I think.

I'm drawn to the space between what data tells us
and what we're yet to figure out.

A Data Science graduate, a builder of curious little systems, and someone who rarely takes a model's first answer at face value. I enjoy pulling problems apart, finding patterns in unexpected places, and seeing how far an idea can go when you give it the right tools.

At Digitalis Global, I've found myself working with the commercial side of data — from Google Analytics and Meta Ads to CRM behaviour, competitor research, website audits and revenue insights. It's given me a different appreciation for data: numbers are interesting, but what they reveal about people, decisions and businesses is even more so.

One of my favourite challenges was CLIO, my university capstone. I led a team of three to explore AI-generated video advertising through the lens of storytelling and Greek mythology. We experimented with CogVideoX-5B, LoRA fine-tuning and a hand-curated collection of 200 videos, working with whatever GPU resources we could get our hands on. We got experimental results, presented our work at the COMSATS exhibition, and learned a fair bit about the distance between an ambitious idea and a deployable system. Its hybrid recommendation engine remains independently accessible.

Since then, I've continued building and experimenting — HydroSight · TouchStone · FraudLab · TabNet · RAG research assistant — each giving me a different reason to question assumptions, test approaches and build something tangible.

I'm particularly fascinated by machine learning, statistical thinking, intelligent systems and the little details that make an idea actually work.

I like my data questioned, my experiments reproducible, my systems thoughtful and my coffee probably stronger than necessary.

I don't just look for patterns. I want to understand why they're there.
