Tabular DL · Research Study project

TabNet vs conventional tabular baselines

An interpretability-focused study: can TabNet predict customer churn on the Telco dataset while providing useful feature-level explanations — and how does it behave under class imbalance versus Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost?

Dataset

Telco Customer Churn — 7,043 customers, 19 predictors after removing customer ID, binary churn target (~26.5% churn rate). Fixed 80/20 stratified train-test split.

Experimentation

  • Baselines: Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost (45 processed features via scaling + one-hot)
  • TabNet & Weighted TabNet on the original 19 features with categorical embeddings
  • Focus on class-imbalance behavior and attention-based feature masks

Why it matters

Most tabular work defaults to tree ensembles. This study isolates whether sequential attention in TabNet adds interpretability value on a classic churn problem — without overselling production readiness.

Next iteration

Deeper reporting of calibrated metrics across imbalance strategies, and clearer visualization of TabNet attention masks alongside SHAP/feature importances from baselines.
